Can ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini tell whether a song was made by AI?

Short answer

Not from the sound. They only "detect" AI when the file's tags say so or when the lyrics give it away.

Question

People upload songs and ask a chatbot "is this AI?" Do the answers mean anything?

Songs

15 AI songs (Suno, Udio) and 10 human songs, in pairs matched by style.

Method

5 experiments, each removing a giveaway or making the AI easier to spot. A fresh chat for every song.

Check

131 verdicts scored against the truth, with each model's stated confidence.

01 The songs

Real AI songs against human songs in the same style

Each AI song was paired with a human song in the same style, so genre alone can't give the answer away. The pairs were chosen to be hard: we can't reliably tell them apart by ear either.

AI-madeAI

  • Experiments 1–3: 5 songs from Suno v5 / v5.5, the current generator. Mostly instrumental: lo-fi, house-pop, trap, afro house, dub techno.
  • Experiment 4: 5 songs from older generators (Suno v3.5, Udio), all with vocals, music and lyrics written by AI.
  • Experiment 5: 5 more older-generator Suno songs with vocals, but with lyrics written by people.
  • Sources: public datasets (Kukedlc Suno dataset; SONICS).

Human-madeHuman

  • Experiments 1–3: 5 tracks matching the AI styles above.
  • Experiments 4–5: 5 tracks with vocals: house-pop, club, R&B, synthpop, dubstep.
  • All released under Creative Commons on netlabels and ccMixter, before AI music generators existed, so they are certainly human.
  • Little-known artists, so a model can't simply recognise a famous song.

Experiments 1→3 remove giveaways one by one. 4 and 5 swap in AI songs that should be easier to spot. Full details are in How it was done.

  • "Detection" is reading the label. ChatGPT and Claude open the file with code and find made with suno or the artist's tags. Remove the tags (experiment 2) and they drop straight to chance, mostly answering "AI" for everything.
  • Gemini is the only one that listens, and it scores about 6/10 at every level. It leans "human" and justifies it with stock phrases like "clean transients, natural groove".
  • Experiment 4 looked like a breakthrough: Gemini ranked every AI song above every human one. But it cited the lyrics every time, and those lyrics were AI-written, often about AI. With human-written lyrics (experiment 5) the same kind of AI music fell to chance.
  • ChatGPT sounds as if it listens but doesn't. It said "no vocals" on 7 of 10 songs with vocals and called a hip-hop beat "cinematic orchestral".
  • High frequencies didn't help. In a side test, a human song played at full quality was still called AI by all three models (details).

05 How it was done

The five experiments

Experiments 1–3 use the same 10 songs and remove one kind of giveaway at a time. Experiments 4–5 change the songs instead, to give the models their best chance.

An earlier pilot on the same 10 songs ran only the experiment 3 condition (single turn, plus variants with full songs and ChatGPT's thinking mode). Every variant landed at chance, which is what prompted this graded design.

Each test is a two-turn chat

  • Turn 1, describe the file (AI is not mentioned): tempo, instruments, vocals, length, sample rate, any tags, and how the model accessed the audio ("listened", "code analysis", ...). This shows whether it can perceive the file at all.
  • Turn 2, the verdict, in the same chat: "AI" or "human", a confidence from 50 to 100%, the main basis for the decision and up to five pieces of evidence.
  • Fresh chat per song (temporary chat, or memory off). ChatGPT: Think off. Claude: Sonnet 5.5, medium effort. Gemini: Flash.

  • The chat apps don't show token counts, so tokens are estimated from the text on the page (about 4 characters per token). Gemini turns the audio itself into tokens (32 per second, sent with both turns); ChatGPT and Claude keep the audio in a code sandbox.
  • Cost uses one illustrative rate, $3 per million tokens in and $15 per million out, not any provider's price list. The runs themselves cost nothing beyond existing subscriptions.

Caveats

  • Small: 10 songs per experiment, one run each. This is a smoke test, not a precise measurement.
  • Consumer apps: model versions are chosen by the app and can change without notice.
  • Our processing shows up in the evidence: in experiment 3 Claude cited the low-pass filter and the fade that our clean-up adds to every clip. These are identical for AI and human songs, so they can't help, but they do distort the reasoning.
  • Experiments 4–5 use 16 kHz mono audio for every file, and experiment 5 is all Suno (the dataset has no Udio songs with human lyrics).
  • Vocal/instrumental labels come from metadata and weren't checked by ear. A few chats were redone after upload failures; this is noted in the logs.
